01

The context

ARR and nature-based solutions monitoring brings together observations with different spatial resolutions, dates, and quality constraints. The geospatial workstream needed to translate monitoring requirements into usable data structures, analytical workflows, and internal outputs.

02

My contribution

I led the geospatial workstream from initial requirements to internal use. This included interpreting business requirements, setting technical priorities, designing spatial data structures, and initially implementing geospatial processing workflows on GCP and the PostGIS database schema. I also led a team of three GIS analysts.

03

The analytical work

I built Python and Google Earth Engine pipelines for NDVI, SAVI, and MSAVI time series, land-cover classification, change detection, and parcel-level carbon monitoring. I integrated satellite imagery, drone data, and GPS/field surveys with spatial QA/QC, maps, and technical documentation.

04

From initial implementation to engineering handoff

My role included the initial system design and implementation. I handed off system designs to engineers for further scaling and refinement. This distinction matters: the case describes my geospatial workstream and the initial implementation, alongside the engineering contribution to its subsequent development.

05

Making imagery usable

I implemented a drone-imagery tiling workflow that reduced load time from approximately 2–3 minutes to a few seconds. This is an approximate operational observation from the work, rather than a standardized performance benchmark or a guarantee for every imagery dataset.

06

Delivery and limits

The workstream reached internal use and produced analytical workflows, maps, spatial quality checks, and documentation supporting monitoring and verification readiness. These outputs support a verification process; they do not by themselves establish verified carbon outcomes or certification.
